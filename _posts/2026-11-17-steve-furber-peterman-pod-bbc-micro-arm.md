Ryan Peterman has published a [Peterman Pod](https://www.developing.dev/p/e075-steve-furber) conversation with Steve Furber, *How Two First-Timers Created the World's Most Popular Processor*. The episode appeared on 16 November 2026. It is also on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3K9L0dRRzOs) and runs for about 56 minutes.

Furber is a former Acorn engineer and, according to his [Wikipedia biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Furber), a principal designer of the BBC Micro and of the ARM 32-bit RISC microprocessor. During his Cambridge PhD in the late 1970s he already did voluntary hardware work for Hermann Hauser and Chris Curry in the company that became Acorn. After finishing that research fellowship he joined Acorn formally in October 1981 as a hardware designer, later design manager, and worked on the final design and production of the BBC Micro. From 1983 he and Sophie Wilson designed ARM; he built a reference model of the first ARM in 808 lines of BBC BASIC, and the first silicon followed 18 months later. In 1990 he left for the University of Manchester.

The episode itself is the place to hear him on that history. Topics include joining Acorn and the BBC Micro, the ARM design, why Acorn failed, and neuromorphic computing, including the SpiNNaker project.
