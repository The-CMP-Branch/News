German quantum computing company planqc announced on 23 November 2026 that it is expanding into the United Kingdom with a new laboratory in Cambridge, backed by Acorn Computers co-founder Hermann Hauser. The company published the news as [planqc expands into the UK with new Cambridge site](https://planqc.eu/news/planqc-expands-into-the-uk-with-new-cambridge-site). [The Telegraph](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2026/11/23/founder-of-britains-biggest-tech-firm-sets-up-quantum-lab-i/) and [New Electronics](https://www.newelectronics.co.uk/content/news/planqc-establishes-cambridge-base-as-part-of-uk-expansion) also reported the announcement.

planqc is based in Garching, near Munich, and grew out of the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics and LMU Munich. It builds quantum computers that use individual neutral atoms as qubits. It is setting up planqc UK Ltd, with a Cambridge site to assemble, integrate and commission systems, and is investing "double-digit millions" of euros. The company has not given an exact figure, an opening date or a qubit target.

Co-founders Dr Sebastian Blatt and Dr Alexander Glätzle will lead the UK company. planqc will work on optical-lattice technology with Professor Ulrich Schneider's group at the University of Cambridge's Cavendish Laboratory.

The firm has more than 130 staff and has raised €55 million in seed and Series A funding. Together with publicly funded projects — the €29 million DiNAQC contract and the €19.1 million MAQCS consortium — the announced financing and project volumes exceed €100 million. Cambridge is its second site outside Germany, after Innsbruck.

Hauser has been an investor and advisory board member since 2023. On the planqc announcement he said: "With Arm, we demonstrated in Cambridge that superior architecture ultimately wins out. I believe quantum computing will be no different, and neutral atoms in optical lattices are among the approaches I see as having the greatest potential. The fact that planqc is now advancing this architecture at the Cavendish Laboratory, where I completed my own PhD, brings things full circle for me." He also said: "Europe has an opportunity not just to use the next major computing platform, but to help shape it."

Hauser co-founded Acorn Computers with Chris Curry in 1978 and, according to [The Telegraph](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2026/11/23/founder-of-britains-biggest-tech-firm-sets-up-quantum-lab-i/), was part of the team that spun Arm out of Acorn in 1990. He was a co-founder and leader of Acorn when Sophie Wilson and Steve Furber designed the ARM architecture at the company.
