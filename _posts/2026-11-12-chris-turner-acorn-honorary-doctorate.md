Anglia Ruskin University (ARU) has awarded Christopher Turner an Honorary Doctor of Science at a graduation ceremony in Cambridge. ARU's [press release](https://www.aru.ac.uk/news/honorary-degree-for-pioneer-of-computing-revolution) of 11 November 2026, headed "Honorary degree for pioneer of computing revolution", describes the ARU alumnus as the first engineer at Cambridge's Acorn Computers.

*Computer engineer Christopher Turner after receiving his honorary degree in Cambridge. Photo: Anglia Ruskin University.*

Turner started work in 1967 at the University of Cambridge's Cavendish Laboratory. He was employed as a crystallography technician, then built logic boards for a PDP-11, and later moved into the main electronics workshop, where he met Hermann Hauser. In 1978 Hauser asked him to work on a computer for Clive Sinclair's Science of Cambridge; that project became the MK14 kit, which Turner developed in his evenings and weekends.

At the end of 1978 Hauser co-founded CPU Ltd and appointed Turner as its first employee and Chief Engineer. The firm became Acorn Computers. Over the following decade Acorn produced the Acorn Atom microcomputer and the BBC Micro, and ARU places Turner among the engineers of the Cambridge Phenomenon alongside fellow Acorn alumni Steve Furber, Andy Hopper and Sophie Wilson.

After Olivetti acquired Acorn in the mid-1980s, Turner became Operations Manager at Olivetti Research Laboratory. He contributed to work on Asynchronous Transfer Mode (ATM) switches and to the 1993 spin-out of Virata, where he served as Vice-President of Operations and IT for seven years. In 2010 he joined Arm, the microprocessor company that grew out of Acorn, and worked on defining safety-critical processors. ARU says that as Director of Product Marketing and Emerging Technologies he helped define the Cortex-R series used in autonomous vehicles, robotics and aerospace.

In his talk [Acorn Computers Employee #1 - Chris Turner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=itGd11j3HdI) at The National Museum of Computing, Turner said he was at Acorn for just under ten years, then Olivetti Research, Virata, Cambridge Consultants and Arm, retiring in 2018.

At the Cambridge ceremony he credited lecturing staff at the Cambridge College of Arts and Technology — one of ARU's predecessor institutions — who had taught him some fifty years earlier, and he addressed 237 graduates. Professor Marcian Cirstea, Head of ARU's School of Computing and Information Science, praised him as a role model for students. [Essex Ledger](https://essexledger.co.uk/2026/11/christopher-turner-receives-honorary-doctor-of-science-from-anglia-ruskin-university/) also reported the award on 11 November, citing ARU as its source.
