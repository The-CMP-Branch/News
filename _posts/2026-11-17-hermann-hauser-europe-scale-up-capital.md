Daniel Postico, writing from London, has published an interview with Hermann Hauser in the Spanish newspaper [El Confidencial](https://www.elconfidencial.com/mundo/2026-11-11/tecnologia-europa-pierde-startups-eeuu-china_4462143/) on 11 November 2026, on why Europe loses start-ups to the United States and China.

Hauser co-founded Acorn Computers with Chris Curry in 1978 and later co-founded the venture firm Amadeus Capital Partners, according to his [Wikipedia biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Hauser). He was a co-founder and leader of Acorn when Sophie Wilson and Steve Furber designed the ARM architecture at the company.

Hauser told El Confidencial he agrees with Mario Draghi's diagnosis of European competitiveness. In a remark translated from Spanish, he said "Draghi is right" and that "the problem is action, implementation" — Europe is good at analysing its problems, less so at acting on them.

Europe's difficulty, he said, is not talent or universities but scale-up capital. European universities produce intellectual property and start-ups on a similar scale to the United States; the gap appears when companies try to expand. Europe holds roughly €15 trillion in pension and insurance funds, but only about 0.02% goes to venture capital. He said 1–2% would solve the funding problem.

He sits on the board of a new European scale-up fund launched about a month before the interview. When it was presented it already had nine investments. It had raised €5 billion, expected soon to reach €7 billion, with about 80% private money. The aim is €100 billion, against a predicted €800 billion scale-up funding gap.

Hauser regards the sale of ARM to SoftBank as a big loss for Europe. ARM listed about three years ago at around $50 billion and is now worth more than $250 billion. An Nvidia takeover, he said, would have been worse; in a remark translated from Spanish, he called ARM "the Switzerland of microchips".

He recalled Microsoft's Nathan Myhrvold asking for his help in the 1990s to set up Microsoft's Cambridge research laboratory. He named Munich, Berlin, Barcelona, Stockholm, Helsinki and Vienna as cities catching up with Silicon Valley.
