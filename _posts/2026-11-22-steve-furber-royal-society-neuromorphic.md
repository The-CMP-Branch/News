The Royal Society is holding [Innovating technologies for neuromorphic computing](https://royalsociety.org/science-events-and-lectures/2026/11/neuromorphic-computing/) at the Royal Society, London, and online this week. A discussion meeting runs on 23–24 November 2026, followed by a "Transforming our Future" day on 25 November.

The organisers are Professor Stephen Furber CBE FREng FRS (University of Manchester), with Professor Judith Driscoll FREng FRS, Professor Stuart Parkin FREng FRS, Dr Sebastian C Dixon and Professor Tony Kenyon. The meeting is free. In-person tickets are sold out, with a waiting list; online registration remains open.

On Monday 23 November, after the 09:00 welcome, Furber gives the 09:05–09:30 talk "Neuromorphic computing in the Human Brain Project". The published abstract covers SpiNNaker, BrainScaleS and the energy cost of AI. He chairs Session 7 (13:40–15:15) on the Transforming our Future day.

Furber worked at Acorn Computers, where he was a principal designer of the BBC Micro and, with Sophie Wilson, of the original ARM processor, according to his [Wikipedia biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Furber) and the Royal Society's speaker note.
